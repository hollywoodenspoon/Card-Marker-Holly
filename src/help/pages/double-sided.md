# Double-sided printing

When your cards have backs, Hearthside Cardworks makes a backs sheet for every fronts sheet. It is laid out so each back lands exactly behind its own front when the page is printed on both sides.

## How the backs sheet works

Turning a sheet over swaps its sides, so the backs sheet is a mirror image of the fronts sheet. With the usual flip, a card in the top-left corner of the fronts sheet has its back in the top-right corner of the backs sheet. The backs themselves stay the right way up.

Cut marks and guide lines are mirrored too, so they meet the marks on the front.

In the print preview, the backs are labelled, for example, "Backs (mirrored, long-edge flip)". A card without a back template leaves an empty spot on the backs sheet.

## Long edge or short edge

Printers turn the paper over in one of two ways, and the backs sheet must match. In the print view, under **Backs**, choose **Double-sided flip (match your printer)**:

- **Long edge**: the sheet turns over like a book page. Most printers do this by default.
- **Short edge**: the sheet turns over like a notepad flipped upwards.

Choose the same setting in your printer's dialog when you print (often called "Flip on long edge" or "Flip on short edge"). If the two don't match, the backs end up on the wrong cards.

## The alignment test sheet

Before printing a whole deck, print the test page once:

1. In the print view, press **Alignment test** (on a phone, **Test page**). It saves a two-page PDF using your current paper, orientation, margin and flip.
2. Print it double-sided at 100% scale, with the same flip setting.
3. Hold the sheet up to a window or a lamp. The front has five crosshairs, marked A to E: one near each corner and one in the middle. The back has the same crosshairs, plus small mm scales to the right of and below each one.
4. If the crosshairs on both sides sit on top of each other, your printer is aligned and you are ready to print.

The grey boxes on the test sheet show where the cards will go.

## Fixing an offset

Many printers shift the back side by a millimetre or so. You can correct for that:

1. On the back of the test sheet, read on the mm scales how far the front crosshair (seen through the paper) sits from the back one. The ticks are 0.5 mm apart.
2. Enter the difference as **Back offset X** (left and right) and **Back offset Y** (up and down), from −10 to 10 mm, in steps of 0.1 mm. Positive X moves the backs right and positive Y moves them down, looking at the back with its "BACK" label the right way up.
3. Print the alignment test again to check. The offset applies to the test sheet too.

The offset is saved with the project, so you only need to do this once per printer.

> **Note:** If the offset changes from print to print, the printer is feeding the paper unevenly. Leave a little more bleed or keep important art away from the edges.

## Printing backs separately

**Sheets** under **Backs** picks what goes into the PDF:

- **Fronts + backs** (default): fronts sheet 1, backs sheet 1, fronts sheet 2, and so on.
- **Fronts**: fronts only.
- **Backs**: backs only, still mirrored for your flip setting.

With **Backs** you can print without a duplex printer. Print the fronts, put the printed sheets back in the paper tray, and print the backs onto their other side. Test which way round your printer needs the paper with the alignment sheet first.
